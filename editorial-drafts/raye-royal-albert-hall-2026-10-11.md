# RAYE Announces One-Night Orchestral Show at Royal Albert Hall

**Status:** LIVE on music98.news. Corrected copy published and verified in the authenticated editor.

**Excerpt (plain-text rendering of the first sentence):** RAYE will return to London's Royal Albert Hall on October 23 to perform her second album in full.

## Article body

RAYE will return to London's Royal Albert Hall on October 23 to perform her second album in full. The one-night concert, *This Symphony May Contain Hope.*, will feature her band, the Heritage Orchestra and Flames Collective choir. It will reunite the musicians behind her 2023 symphonic show at the same venue. Tickets go on sale October 15 at 10am.

Released in March, *This Music May Contain Hope.* unfolds across four seasons, beginning with loneliness and gradually moving towards hope. RAYE told Apple Music she wanted to make songs people could turn to during difficult times. For "Click Clack Symphony.", she sent Hans Zimmer a rough demo and asked if he wanted to work on it. The two exchanged ideas until the recording included an orchestra. She later described the result as "unapologetically cinematic." The album became her first UK No. 1.

The announcement also came with a new performance of "I Will Overcome." recorded at Abbey Road Studios with the London Symphony Orchestra. In January, RAYE played the album version for an ELLE journalist and called it "big and dramatic." She pointed to lyrics that ask whether people hide their pain, then admit she isn't okay but believes she will eventually get through it. The song appears near the beginning of the record, following a short opening scene about being alone in Paris.

That earlier Royal Albert Hall show was filmed and released as a concert movie and live album. The film earned a Grammy nomination for Best Music Film, and RAYE took a similar production to The O2 the following year. For the performance, she rearranged songs from *My 21st Century Blues* for orchestra and choir. RAYE told the Recording Academy she enjoyed changing songs for the stage, sometimes extending introductions or switching styles because she wanted the audience to hear something different each time.

Getting that first album released had been a struggle of its own. RAYE spent years writing for other artists while her own singles leaned towards dance-pop, despite her interest in making R&B. She has said she had little creative control at Polydor, her record label at the time. After speaking publicly about years of delays, she left in 2021 and eventually released her music independently. On the album, she could explore the jazz and soul influences that had received less attention in her earlier work.

After leaving Polydor, RAYE released "Escapism." with 070 Shake. The song follows someone trying to numb the pain of a breakup through drinking and nights out, a far darker subject than many of her earlier dance-pop singles. Its success took her to No. 1 in the UK before the album was released. In an interview with ELLE, she described that moment as proof that she had been right to back herself.

RAYE has described a different approach to writing her latest album. She told ELLE that she made most of it sober and had stopped using alcohol to bury difficult feelings. Its opening autumn chapter, she explained, deals with looking for quick fixes in relationships or a drink. She was learning instead to spend time by herself, without expecting another person to make everything better. "I don't want someone else to come and fix me," she said.
