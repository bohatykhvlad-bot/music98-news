# The Ella Langley Phenomenon and Country's New Boom

Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas" broke the Hot 100's No. 1 longevity record with a steel-guitar breakup song made for a country dance floor. Country radio embraced it first, and pop stations took months to catch up. Its narrator takes her boyfriend to Texas and discovers that he has never quite left another woman behind. The song reached listeners far beyond country radio without losing its honky-tonk sound. The unlikely writing session behind that unhappy story began with Miranda Lambert's pet kangaroo.

At their first songwriting retreat together, Miranda Lambert told Langley and fellow writers Luke Dick and Joybeth Taylor about the time she was pulled over with her pet kangaroo in the passenger seat and a dog in the back. Lambert happened to mention her Texas license plates. Langley heard the start of a lyric in that detail, while Lambert suggested that the Texas woman could be the one a man had chosen over his girlfriend. Langley recalled at Billboard Women in Music that they finished the song about 45 minutes later. The animal disappeared from the finished lyric, but Lambert's license plate gave the writers their Texas setting and the words that set the chorus in motion.

The lyric gives the listener time to catch up with the woman telling the story. She thought she'd persuaded her boyfriend to love Tennessee, only to take him back to Abilene and watch him come alive around somebody else. Even his enthusiasm for George Strait's "Amarillo by Morning" looks different in retrospect. He is dancing, the people around them are enjoying the night, and she can see that his feelings have never quite left Texas. By the bridge, she is imagining the eastbound miles of I-40 and admitting she cannot change his mind. The recording leaves her with that painful prospect rather than showing a completed departure.

The record has a way of making the bad news easy to dance to. Its drums and bass keep a steady, midtempo step while electric guitar and pedal steel give the melody its country edges. Nothing in the backing stops or turns mournful when the narrator catches sight of the other woman. Instead, the chorus opens up into a hook that sounds just as natural in a crowded bar as it does coming from a car radio. Listen again after the lyrics have sunk in, though, and that comfortable rhythm begins to sound almost indifferent to the person singing over it. Langley told Billboard that when she first hears a song, she listens to the track and instrumentation before the words. "Choosin' Texas" rewards both kinds of attention: its musical ease on a first listen, then the unhappiness moving beneath it.

Langley moved to Nashville in 2019 after growing up near Hope Hull, Alabama, with country music playing at home. She began performing cover sets in bars at 18 and spent years carrying her own songs from one small venue to another. A bar full of strangers is also the setting for her breakthrough duet with Riley Green, where a conversation, rather than an elaborate romantic confession, does almost all the work. That earlier recording would give her a very different kind of hit from "Choosin' Texas."

Miranda Lambert had mattered to Langley long before they shared a writing room. Langley has recalled turning to Lambert's "The House That Built Me" after her family lost their own home. The singer behind that record eventually became a co-writer on "Choosin' Texas," then co-produced the single with Langley and Ben West and contributed backing vocals. Their collaboration continued on *Dandelion*. Langley has also spoken about asking Lambert for advice while navigating the industry, drawing on the experience of someone whose country career had already survived the upheaval of sudden fame.

Langley also took a directing credit on the official "Choosin' Texas" video, alongside Wales Toney and photographer Caylee Robillard. They placed the story inside the Stagecoach Ballroom in Fort Worth, with Luke Grimes playing the boyfriend and Ava Phillippe the woman who catches his attention. Lambert appears onstage. The dance hall is full of people who have no reason to notice the relationship falling apart. The characters' glances do much of the work. Where the song imagines the drive away, the film shows Langley's character leaving in a car with Lambert.

The car that takes Langley's character away carries the plate "ICLYA." Langley later confirmed that it refers to "I Can't Love You Anymore," her subsequently released duet with Morgan Wallen.

On her 2024 hit with Riley Green, "you look like you love me," Langley insisted on keeping the spoken verses. The pair play strangers trying to start a conversation in a bar, and neither needs the polished language of a conventional duet to make the exchange entertaining. Langley told Theo Von that her label had wanted her to return to the studio and sing her parts after they had been recorded. She refused. The spoken lines stayed in the finished track, and the two singers sound all the more awkward and funny for it.

[youtube:Dm2TSMerGPQ]

The Riley Green duet and "Choosin' Texas" use the barroom in different ways. In the first, two strangers exchange bold, awkward approaches, and the spoken passages allow both characters a say. In the second, only one person tells us what went wrong. The man and the woman who has caught his eye never get a verse to explain themselves. It is a different songwriting task, achieved without changing the conversational voice that made Langley's earlier hit distinctive. Her next challenge was to perform that more private story in rooms far larger than the ones where her career began.

At CMA Fest in June 2026, the audience knew the chorus of "Choosin' Texas" well enough to sing much of it back. In the official footage, Langley leaves the crowd room to take over, then resumes the next verse while the band keeps playing.

Country radio gave "Choosin' Texas" its first No. 1 in February. The song did not reach the Pop Airplay top 10 until August, by which point its original country audience had been listening for months. That later rise gave the single another route to listeners. By the Hot 100 chart dated October 3, 2026, it had accumulated 24 weeks at No. 1, surpassing the previous record held by Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You."

*Dandelion* ranges more widely than its biggest single might suggest. "Be Her" follows Langley's thoughts about the person she wants to become, while "Butterfly Season" brings Lambert back as a duet partner. The album also includes a short traditional tune and a recording associated with an earlier generation of female country singers. Her version of that older country standard is especially revealing because of the circumstances in which it was first recorded.

[apple:album:1869436835]

Kitty Wells recorded "It Wasn't God Who Made Honky Tonk Angels" in 1952, answering Hank Thompson's "The Wild Side of Life" after his hit laid the blame for broken relationships largely on women. Wells's recording became a country No. 1 and gave the accused woman a voice of her own. Langley's version appears on *Dandelion*, but her interest in Wells extends well beyond the album. She has said she uses the original recording as the alarm on her smart speaker. Wells's singer answers an accusation outright, while the woman in "Choosin' Texas" must reckon with a choice she cannot change.

Dolly Parton's "Jolene" gives its narrator a way to address a possible rival directly. "I Will Always Love You" takes a different approach to saying goodbye. Parton wrote it as she ended her professional partnership with Porter Wagoner, although its words have also resonated with listeners hearing a romantic farewell. Langley's narrator makes neither a plea to the other woman nor a grand declaration to the man. She recognizes where his heart lies and pictures the journey home, even as the loss still hurts.

In 2023, country songs occupied all three top positions on the Billboard Hot 100 in the same week, a first in the chart's history. The records included Luke Combs' version of Tracy Chapman's "Fast Car" alongside hits by Morgan Wallen and Jason Aldean. They were very different records, and the Aldean hit attracted controversy. Langley's success followed an era in which country was already finding its way to the top of the all-genre chart.

The range of artists reaching those listeners became clearer in 2024. Beyoncé's *COWBOY CARTER* renewed debate about country's Black roots and its boundaries, while Shaboozey's genre-mixing "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" spent 19 weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100. Langley's single offered yet another route. Its guitar and steel recalled older dance-hall country, but its easygoing feel also suited playlists that were not organized by genre. Spotify senior editor Claire Heinichen told the Associated Press that "Choosin' Texas" moved from Hot Country into Today's Top Hits and playlists for driving, relaxing and other everyday listening. Those were places where new listeners could find the very same recording that country radio had embraced months earlier.

[youtube:238Z4YaAr1g]

After Langley passed Mariah Carey, online commenters claimed they had never heard the hit, a reaction documented by *The Atlantic*. In an Associated Press interview, music historian Charles L. Hughes offered a possible explanation. The song had reached radio listeners, streaming playlists and TikTok without necessarily becoming a single shared pop-cultural reference. The Hot 100 adds streams, airplay and sales, regardless of how much those audiences overlap. "Choosin' Texas" could dominate the chart while remaining unfamiliar to people whose listening habits rarely crossed those audiences.

Post Malone arrived in country from another direction. He had spent years attracting listeners through hip-hop, pop and rock before performing "I Had Some Help" with Morgan Wallen at Stagecoach and releasing *F-1 Trillion* with country collaborators. His existing fans could follow him into that music. Langley played bars and small venues, built her audience through country music and began reaching mainstream playlists much later.

[youtube:4QIZE708gJ4]

The success has not made opportunities equal, particularly for women in country music. In April, "Be Her" joined "Choosin' Texas" in the Country Airplay top 10, making Langley the first woman to have two solo songs in that part of the chart simultaneously. Lambert, speaking in a 2026 interview, was optimistic about opportunities available to younger women compared with the scene she encountered early in her career. Langley's breakthrough gives that optimism a concrete example, but it does not mean every new female artist has the same access to radio or experienced mentors.

At a September show in Columbus, Ohio, Langley stopped to address criticism of her appearance, speech and performances. She told the audience that the remarks had shaken her enough to wonder whether she could handle the attention. She described traveling between small venues in a van and a Honda Accord, living on bar food and tip-jar money. She said she was proud of the young woman who had kept playing those shows.

Before the vans and tip jars she recalled in Columbus, Langley sat beside her grandfather at his piano and learned "Froggy Went A Courtin'." She has recalled family reunions when everyone gathered around that piano to sing it, and she placed fragments of the tune at both ends of *Dandelion*. The closing recording strips away the full band heard on "Choosin' Texas," leaving her voice with acoustic guitar instead of a sweeping farewell. After the biggest hit of her career brought strangers into her audience, she ends the album with a song her family used to sing together.
